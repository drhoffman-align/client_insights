Therapeutic Insights

Alignment Psychiatry

The Situation

You are employed in a role you genuinely enjoy, yet you face significant anxiety due to persistent workplace hostility from your supervisor that began in February. You're experiencing unclear job expectations, lack of necessary resources, communication isolation, and attempts to undermine your position through shifting goalposts and surveillance. These experiences trigger past trauma related to bullying and discrimination, creating distress despite your professional competence and appropriate use of FMLA accommodations.

Core Problem

"You are experiencing workplace gaslighting and a hostile environment designed to pressure your resignation. Your supervisor creates unpredictability through unclear communication, shifting expectations, and personal attacks, causing self-doubt despite clear evidence of your effectiveness and professionalism."

Clinical Concepts

Gaslighting: A form of manipulation that causes you to doubt your own perception and reality through shifting expectations and false accusations. When someone consistently changes the rules and then blames you for not following them, they're attempting to destabilize your confidence in your own judgment.
Fight/Flight Response: Your natural anxiety reaction to perceived threat. In workplace settings, "fighting" doesn't mean aggression—it means using evidence and process strategically to protect yourself and assert your rights.
Professional Boundaries: Clear, documented interactions that protect you against retaliation. Boundaries aren't walls; they're guidelines that define respectful, accountable communication and behavior.
Power Dynamics: In dysfunctional workplaces, authority is often derived from controlling information and process, not just from titles. When you document and follow proper procedures, you reclaim power through transparency and accountability.

Therapeutic Frameworks

Evidence-Based Defense Framework

This framework shifts power from emotional reactivity to strategic action, using information and process as protection in dysfunctional workplaces.

1
Document Everything

Create a detailed record of all interactions, promises, expectations, and incidents. Include dates, times, witnesses, and exact language used. Documentation transforms subjective experiences into objective evidence.

2
Know Your Rights

Familiarize yourself with company policies, union protections, FMLA regulations, and workplace discrimination laws. Knowledge of your rights provides a foundation for confident action.

3
Direct Confrontation

Address issues professionally and directly, asking for clarification on expectations and pointing out inconsistencies. This clarifies intentions and creates a record of your attempts to resolve issues.

4
Paper Trail Maintenance

Follow up all meetings and conversations with recap emails. This creates accountability and prevents "he said/she said" situations. Written communication becomes your protection.

5
Maintain Professionalism

Regardless of provocation, remain calm, factual, and professional in all interactions. Your composure and professionalism contrast with unprofessional behavior and strengthen your position.

6
Seek Support

Utilize available support systems including union representatives, HR, and trusted colleagues. Isolation is a tactic used in hostile environments; connection is your counter-strategy.

Practical Instructions

Preparing for Your Next Interaction:

  • Before any meeting with your supervisor or HR, prepare a written list of specific questions and concerns
  • Bring your documentation timeline to reference during discussions
  • Take notes during meetings or ask if you may record for accuracy
  • Within 24 hours of any meeting, send a recap email summarizing what was discussed and agreed upon
  • If expectations are given verbally, ask for them in writing or send your understanding via email for confirmation
  • Set firm boundaries on communication during approved FMLA leave—you are not required to respond to work matters during protected time off
  • When confronted with criticism, ask for specific examples and documentation rather than accepting vague accusations

Homework

Complete before your next session or return to work:

  • Review all recent communications and meetings with your supervisor
  • Create a chronological timeline noting promises made versus actual outcomes
  • Identify specific inconsistencies in management actions and expectations
  • Draft an evidence-supported summary of your perspective on the situation
  • Prepare a list of clear, direct questions for your next supervisor or HR meeting
  • Identify at least two support resources (union rep, HR contact, trusted colleague) you can reach out to

Key Takeaways

  • Documentation transforms anxiety into strategic power by creating objective evidence
  • Direct, professional confrontation clarifies intentions and provides legal protection
  • Evidence serves as your primary defense tool against gaslighting and false accusations
  • Maintain professionalism regardless of provocation—your composure is your strength
  • Utilize support systems (union, HR) actively to prevent isolation tactics
  • Set firm boundaries on communication during approved leave periods
  • Send recap emails after all meetings to maintain accountability and create a paper trail
  • The problem reflects others' dysfunction, not your competence or worth
  • Recognize that attacks often target your strengths because they threaten those who feel insecure
  • Prepare for all meetings with evidence and clear objectives to stay grounded and focused
Bottom Line: You are facing a hostile work environment designed to make you doubt yourself and resign. Your anxiety is a natural response to real dysfunction, not a reflection of your capabilities. By documenting everything, knowing your rights, maintaining professionalism, and using available support systems, you shift from a defensive position to one of strategic power. The evidence you gather protects you, clarifies reality, and provides the foundation for whatever action you choose to take. Remember: your competence is not in question—the system's integrity is.
