Therapeutic Insights

Alignment Psychiatry  |  Generalized Anxiety Disorder & Work-Related Stress

The Situation

You are currently on medical leave for anxiety and have been experiencing escalating stress due to repeated documentation requests from your supervisor — requests your leave administrator has confirmed are not required. This boundary violation is triggering acute anxiety attacks and reinforcing a cycle of anticipatory dread about returning to work.

Alongside this, your sleep is significantly disrupted: late-night screen use is delaying sleep onset, nightmares are interrupting rest, and low morning energy is compounding daily functioning. These patterns are interconnected and are sustaining your current anxiety symptoms.

Core Problem

The central issue is a collision between an external stressor you cannot fully control — a supervisor who repeatedly crosses professional and legal boundaries — and internal patterns that make it harder to protect yourself: difficulty asserting limits, people-pleasing tendencies, and a nervous system already primed for high alert by chronic sleep deprivation.

This combination keeps anxiety elevated even when the immediate threat is not present, because the anticipation of conflict becomes as activating as the conflict itself.

Understanding the Dynamics

Anticipatory Anxiety The body responds to imagined future threats as if they are happening now, sustaining stress even during calm periods.
Revenge Wakefulness Staying up late for personal time after a stressful day — this feels restorative but systematically erodes sleep quality and next-day resilience.
People-Pleasing Under Pressure Difficulty declining unreasonable requests leads to boundary erosion and reinforces anxiety by signaling that the threat is legitimate.
Stress–Sleep–Anxiety Loop Poor sleep amplifies anxiety, anxiety disrupts sleep — each worsening the other unless one link in the cycle is actively interrupted.

Frameworks

1. Structured Sleep Reset

1
Choose a fixed bedtime and wake time that allow 8–9 hours nightly.
2
Set a hard screen-off point 45–60 minutes before bedtime.
3
Replace screen time with a low-stimulation wind-down activity (reading, stretching, dim light).
4
Keep the wake time consistent even after a poor night — this anchors your circadian rhythm.
Application: Pick your target times before the next session and treat them as a medical appointment — non-negotiable by default.

 

2. Boundary Layering at Work

1
Identify your non-negotiable limits (e.g., work hours end at 1:30 PM, no unscheduled supervisor calls).
2
Write a brief, neutral script for asserting each limit without over-explaining.
3
Rehearse the script mentally or aloud before high-stakes interactions.
4
Debrief after each interaction — note what held and what needs adjustment.
Application: At the next session, bring one specific scenario where you anticipate needing a boundary so we can build a script together.

Practical Instructions

  • Stop screens (phone, TV, laptop) at least 45 minutes before your chosen bedtime — without exception.
  • Use Klonopin only as prescribed; track each use to build awareness of patterns and reduce reliance over time.
  • Keep work communication within your designated part-time hours; do not respond to supervisor contact outside those hours during leave.
  • Build a brief morning routine (10–15 minutes) for personal development or movement before the day's demands begin.

Assignment

Before the next session, decide on a specific bedtime and wake-up time that realistically allows 8–9 hours of sleep, and begin following it tonight. Reflect on what your morning routine could look like — even a small consistent ritual — and come prepared to discuss one boundary situation at work you want to address when you return.

Key Takeaways

Sleep is treatment.Consistent, adequate sleep is a primary intervention, not a bonus.
Anticipation is not prediction.Dreading an event does not mean the worst outcome is inevitable.
Boundaries protect health.Limits on work hours are a clinical necessity, not a preference.
Screen time costs sleep.Late-night screens delay and fragment sleep regardless of how relaxing they feel.
Leave is active recovery.This period exists to stabilize — use it deliberately, not passively.
Rescue meds are a signal.Increased benzodiazepine use indicates the need for earlier behavioral intervention, not escalation.

Bottom Line

Your anxiety is responding to real, ongoing stressors — a supervisor who is not respecting your leave and a sleep deficit that is leaving your nervous system without adequate recovery time. These are solvable problems, but they require consistent behavioral change alongside any medication support.

The work ahead is about reclaiming structure: reliable sleep, clear limits, and a return to work on your terms and timeline. Each small step toward that structure reduces the anxiety load on its own.
