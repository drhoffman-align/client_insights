Alignment Psychiatry

Reclaiming Purpose

Breaking the Cycle of Self-Sabotage and Rediscovering Motivation

The Situation

You are currently on a three-month FMLA break and working to use this time productively — returning to music production, learning new skills, and maintaining physical and mental wellness. Despite clear intentions, you're experiencing procrastination, low motivation, and a sense of heaviness when engaging with your craft as a DJ.

These struggles connect to a longer history of not feeling embraced for your individuality, including early criticism that shaped a deeply ingrained pattern of self-doubt. Inconsistent use of ADHD medication has also made it harder to build and maintain momentum.

Core Problem

The central issue is a persistent cycle of self-sabotage: negative self-talk, procrastination, and dismissing positive feedback. Your internal narrative amplifies criticism while minimizing real evidence of success — repeat bookings, compliments, ongoing demand for your work.

This mismatch between how you feel ("I'm a waste") and what is actually true creates emotional distress and erodes motivation. Unrealistic expectations about how audiences should respond compound this further.

Understanding the Dynamics

Self-SabotageThoughts or behaviors that undermine your own success — here showing up as procrastination, negative self-talk, and discounting positive feedback.
Cognitive DistortionPatterns of thinking that warp reality, such as all-or-nothing thinking or selectively ignoring evidence that contradicts a negative belief.
Evidence-Based AffirmationsPositive statements that work only when they are specific and believable — tied to real proof, not generic encouragement.
Purpose & IdentityA clear, personal "why" behind your work that anchors motivation and provides resilience against external criticism and internal doubt.

Frameworks

1 — CBT: Beliefs, Perceptions, and Evidence
  • 1Identify the automatic belief (e.g., "I'm a waste," "They're lying to me").
  • 2Examine real-world evidence that supports or contradicts that belief.
  • 3Update the meaning — integrate positive evidence rather than discarding it.
  • 4Build a specific, believable affirmation from the updated evidence.
  • 5Repeat consistently to rewire habitual thinking over time.
How it applies You receive repeat bookings and compliments but interpret them as insincere. This framework gives you a structured way to stop discarding that evidence and start letting it count.
2 — Purpose and Identity Clarification
  • 1Define your purpose in your own words (e.g., "I DJ to feed people's souls with new music").
  • 2Align decisions and practice with that purpose, not with pleasing everyone.
  • 3Keep your purpose visible and review it before every gig or practice session.
How it applies When criticism hits, your purpose becomes the anchor. Audience reactions are naturally varied — most fall in the middle, with a few outliers on either end. One negative reaction does not define your performance.

Practical Instructions

  • Journal with processing: After expressing feelings, ask — "Is this true? What evidence do I have for and against this thought?"
  • Post affirmations: Write one specific, evidence-based affirmation and place it somewhere you'll see it daily.
  • Review your purpose: Read your purpose statement before each gig or practice session — not after.
  • Medication consistency: Take ADHD medication twice daily (morning and midday) with food or a meal replacement shake; set phone reminders if needed.

Assignment

Each day until your next session, spend 5–10 minutes writing in your journal — both what went well and what evidence supports your strengths as a DJ. Write your purpose statement in your own words, post it somewhere visible (mirror, phone background), and write at least one specific affirmation to repeat daily. Bring your journal reflections to the next session.

Key Takeaways

Self-sabotage is driven by dismissing real evidence of your success.
Procrastination and avoidance are symptoms of deeper self-doubt, not laziness.
Repeat bookings and compliments are valid proof — let them count.
Audience reactions are diverse; one critic does not represent the whole room.
Affirmations only work when they are specific, believable, and evidence-based.
Purpose guides creative work; pleasing everyone is not a sustainable goal.

Bottom Line

This session focused on how beliefs shaped by past criticism — and reinforced by current habits — distort your perception of your own success. Learning to recognize when you're discarding positive evidence is the first step toward a more balanced, grounded self-view. Anchoring your motivation in purpose, rather than in universal approval, is what makes that shift sustainable.

The most immediate priorities are consistent medication use, daily affirmation practice, and journaling that goes beyond expression into active evidence review. Change happens through repetition. Every time you choose to let the evidence in, you are reinforcing a more accurate — and more useful — story about who you are.
