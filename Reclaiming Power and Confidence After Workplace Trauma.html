Reclaiming Power: Valuing Yourself After Workplace Trauma

Understanding your strengths, rebuilding confidence, and redirecting energy inward

The Situation

Following a job loss marked by workplace bullying, a lack of transparency, and possible retaliation for self-advocacy, the client finds herself emotionally depleted and disconnected from her own sense of motivation and power. She has since secured new contract work — a testament to her professional standing — but recognizes a deep need for rest and self-directed care.

As a highly competent attorney, her professional skills are not in question. The difficulty lies in transferring that competence into relationship-based settings and self-advocacy, areas where she tends to underestimate herself and over-invest in others at her own expense.

Core Problem

The central issue is a significant gap between her actual capabilities — which are high — and her self-perception outside of work. Years of overextending herself for others have produced chronic depletion, and the negative workplace experience has reinforced a tendency to internalize setbacks as personal failures rather than situational injustice.

She finds it easier to show up fully for others than for herself. Until that imbalance shifts, her energy, confidence, and sense of personal power will remain constrained.

Understanding the Dynamics

Confidence vs. ArroganceConfidence is grounded in real evidence of your abilities; arrogance is not. Fear of appearing arrogant often leads capable people to underestimate themselves.
Chronic DepletionSustained emotional exhaustion caused by consistently giving to others without adequate replenishment. It erodes motivation and self-directed action over time.
RetaliationWhen an employer penalizes an employee for asserting their rights. Standing up despite consequences reflects integrity and courage, not a mistake.
Personal BrandThe intentional way you present your strengths publicly. Without it, even strong resources and a solid reputation have limited reach and impact.

Framework 1

Power = Resources × Reputation × Brand

  • 1Identify your Resources — skills, knowledge, and tangible assets you bring.
  • 2Assess your Reputation — how others consistently experience your reliability and competence.
  • 3Build your Brand — make your strengths visible so that opportunities can find you.
How it applies: Legal skills provide a strong resource base, and landing a new job within two days confirms a solid reputation. The underdeveloped piece is brand — putting herself forward more visibly to expand her influence and options.

Framework 2

Exposure as a Learning Strategy

  • 1Engage — try a new experience, group, or type of relationship without needing a guaranteed outcome.
  • 2Observe — notice what fits, what drains, and what energizes.
  • 3Adjust — use what you learned to refine what you seek next.
How it applies: Without many models of healthy relationships or supportive work environments, she has defaulted to self-blame when things go wrong. Deliberate exposure expands her reference points and shifts that pattern.

Practical Instructions

  • Audit your power equation: list your skills, what others say about your work, and one way you could present yourself more publicly.
  • Practice acting with confidence this week — notice where it feels uncomfortable, and notice where it actually works.
  • When you catch yourself doing something for others, pause and ask: what would it look like to do this for myself?
  • Set at least one boundary with a person or situation that consistently drains you — no explanation required.

Assignment

Before the next session, create a written "power equation" using three columns: Resources (skills, knowledge, assets), Reputation (what others have said about your work and character), and Brand (how you might present yourself more visibly or confidently). The goal is not self-promotion — it is clarity. Seeing your strengths on paper is the first step toward acting on them. Bring the document to the next session.

Key Takeaways

Standing up at work was an act of integrity, not a mistake.
Confidence is evidence-based; it is not arrogance.
Depletion is a predictable result of chronic over-giving, not a character flaw.
A strong reputation and skills are assets — a visible brand amplifies them.
Negative outcomes in unhealthy environments are not personal failures.
Redirecting care inward is a skill that develops through practice, not overnight.

Bottom Line

This session centered on recognizing that the depletion and self-doubt she carries are the predictable aftermath of an unjust experience — not evidence of inadequacy. Her capabilities are real and well-documented. The work ahead is learning to direct the same consistency and care she offers others back toward herself.

The immediate priorities are rest, boundary-setting, and completing the power equation exercise. These are not small steps — they represent a genuine reorientation toward her own needs, which is exactly what this stage of recovery requires.
