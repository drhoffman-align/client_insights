💃 Finding Freedom and Balance: Energy, Connection, and Responsibility in Life and Art

Floating and staying grounded aren't opposites — one supports the other

The Situation

Several threads are running at once: embarrassment about not earning enough to invest in the next stage of a business, wariness about being exploited by expensive support services, and a growing awareness of how energy fluctuates in social and travel contexts — including how that pattern differs from a partner's.

Excitement about learning, especially about Italian heritage, brings real energy, while art and movement — particularly dance — remain a primary way of connecting with the unconscious and processing emotion. Deep connection and shared experience are valued, even as social interaction sometimes brings depletion. Journaling and self-reflection continue as an ongoing practice of curiosity about both self and others.

Core Problem

A real tension runs between wanting freedom — to move, create, and connect authentically — and the responsibilities that come with adult life, especially money, business, and relationships. Feeling pulled between wanting to "float like a feather" and being "tethered to the earth" can produce shame, depletion, or self-doubt, especially when financial reality doesn't match internal aspiration.

A pattern of overthinking, of getting "stuck in her head," runs underneath this — something movement and art reliably interrupt. Assumptions about what's possible ("Can I be free all the time?") are being questioned rather than simply accepted.

"You have to have a level of pressure in order to have the freedom you'd like."

Understanding the Dynamics

Autonomic vs. Somatic Nervous System

Most bodily functions run automatically; only a small portion is under conscious control — a physical mirror of how much of life sits outside direct control.

Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs

Love and belonging are foundational before self-esteem and self-actualization become fully reachable. Connection comes first.

ADHD as a Natural State

A brain state that's arguably more natural, with later development of planning and responsibility layered on top over time.

Embodiment as an Antidote to Overthinking

Movement and physical experience interrupt rumination in a way that thinking alone rarely can.

Financial Shame

Embarrassment tied specifically to money and business capacity — common enough to not be mistaken for a personal failing.

Framework A

The Energy-Responsibility Balance Framework

1
Identify what feels natural — activities that feel effortless, energizing, and authentic.
2
Identify responsibilities — tasks that require planning or meeting external standards.
3
Notice the interplay — both freedom and responsibility are necessary; one grounds and supports the other.
4
Seek balance between energy input and output, rest and action, autonomy and connection.
5
Reframe resourcefulness — relationships, creativity, and community are also currencies, not just money.
Applied Dance and movement bring lightness and freedom; business expenses bring heaviness and constraint. Neither has to be resolved away — both are real, necessary parts of the same life.

Practical Instructions

  • Before a social gathering, name in advance roughly how much energy you have to give, so leaving early doesn't require an in-the-moment justification.
  • When a business task feels heavy, do five minutes of movement first and notice whether the task feels different afterward.
  • Once a week, name one person in your life who offered support that wasn't about money, and thank them specifically for it.
  • When you catch yourself asking someone a fourth or fifth deep question in a row, pause and offer something about yourself in return.
  • Keep a short list of experiences that taught you something no amount of reading or planning could have.

Assignment

Track your energy and mood daily — note what energizes you, what depletes you, and any moments you notice both freedom and responsibility being held at once. Use a journal or notes app, and bring your observations to the next session on August 28th.

Key Takeaways

Feeling light during movement and heavy during business tasks isn't inconsistency — it's two different, both-necessary modes operating normally.
A trip cycling between excitement and depletion is evidence of a working rhythm, not a problem to fix.
Support that isn't transactional — a friend's advice, a shared skill — counts as a real resource, not a lesser one.
Shame about business finances is common enough to not be mistaken for a personal failing.
A body seeking rest after expending energy is functioning correctly, not falling behind.
Curiosity directed at others works best paired with occasional openness about yourself.
Understanding gained through doing something firsthand often can't be replicated by thinking it through in advance.
A belief about what's possible for you is a belief, not a fixed fact, and can be tested against actual experience.

Bottom Line

The pull between wanting to float and needing to stay grounded isn't a flaw to resolve. It's the actual structure of a full life, where both directions are necessary and neither one cancels the other out.

Financial strain and the embarrassment that comes with it are common enough that they say very little about capability. The more useful move is widening what counts as a resource beyond money alone.

Tracking energy isn't about eliminating the cycles of excitement and depletion. It's about getting familiar enough with their rhythm that both movement and stillness can be trusted as part of the same, ongoing balance.
