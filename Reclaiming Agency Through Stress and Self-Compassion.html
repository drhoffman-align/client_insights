Reclaiming Agency

Navigating Stress, Power, and Self-Compassion Amidst Life's Ebbs and Flows

The Situation

You are currently managing an unusually heavy load: supporting a spouse through Covid while protecting your own health, navigating ongoing chronic conditions (lupus and a rare blood disorder), and handling friction with insurance and the healthcare system. At the same time, you are sustaining the emotional labor of caretaking and trying to stay connected socially.

At work, you feel disconnected and powerless in the face of systemic barriers. You are experimenting with helpful new habits — journaling, reflective walks — but find it hard to sustain them consistently when your energy is already stretched.

Core Problem

The central difficulty is a felt sense of helplessness — a belief that you cannot meaningfully affect outcomes at home, at work, or in healthcare. This is compounded by a tendency to stay in constant motion ("go, go, go") rather than pausing to process what you're feeling, which makes emotions harder to manage over time.

There is also a significant gap between your internal narrative ("I'm failing," "I shut down") and what is actually happening: you are making adaptive choices every day. Closing that gap is a core focus of the work.

Understanding the Dynamics

Agency

The ability to make choices and act intentionally — even small ones. You have more of this than you currently recognize.

Power

The capacity to force or drive change, often through resources and reputation. You may lack power over systems, but that is different from lacking agency.

Influence

Affecting outcomes collaboratively — through persuasion, relationship, and timing. Less draining than control, and often more effective.

Learned Helplessness

A pattern where repeated uncontrollable events lead to the belief that nothing can change. Recognizing it is the first step toward shifting it.

Framework 1 — Control · Influence · Acceptance

1
Control: Identify what you can directly change — your choices, responses, and actions.
2
Influence: Identify what you can affect indirectly — others' behaviors, immediate environment.
3
Acceptance: Name what is genuinely outside your reach — systems, others' decisions, illness timelines.
4
Focus: Direct energy toward Control and Influence; release effort spent on Acceptance items.
How it applies: Your spouse's illness and work systems fall largely in Acceptance. Your daily routine, your responses, and how you support him fall in Control. Persuading him to take medication is Influence. Sorting these reduces anxiety and clarifies where effort is worth spending.

Framework 2 — Emotional Quick-Capture

1
Notice: Catch the feeling or thought when it arises — don't push it away.
2
Capture: Record it immediately — notes app, voice memo, or a single written phrase.
3
Defer: Set a specific later time to revisit it; don't try to fully process it in the moment.
4
Reflect: During your scheduled time, revisit, reframe, validate, or problem-solve as needed.
How it applies: You currently see taking space as "shutting down." This framework reframes that pause as a healthy, structured coping step — not avoidance, but intentional deferral. Quick capture keeps things from getting lost without requiring you to stop what you're doing.

Practical Instructions

  • When a strong feeling surfaces, capture it in two to three words — don't try to resolve it on the spot.
  • Schedule one 10–15 minute block daily (morning or evening) to review captures and reflect briefly.
  • When you feel stuck, ask: "What is one small choice I can make right now?" — then make it.
  • When self-critical thoughts arise, replace "I shut down" with "I took space because I needed it."

Assignment

This week, use the quick-capture method daily — phone notes, voice memo, or a small notebook. Set aside a consistent 10–15 minute reflection block each day to review what you captured. Begin the Identity Workbook (to be sent separately) and note any reactions. Keep a brief journal on where agency and power show up differently in your life. Bring your captures, workbook impressions, and any observations to the next session.

Key Takeaways

Agency lives in small choices — it doesn't require big outcomes.

Influence through collaboration is more sustainable than seeking control.

Sorting issues into Control / Influence / Acceptance reduces anxiety directly.

Quick capture prevents emotional overload without interrupting your day.

Taking space is adaptive coping, not failure or shutdown.

Learned helplessness can be unlearned — agency is a practiced skill.

Bottom Line

You are carrying a great deal right now, and the feeling of helplessness is an understandable response to genuinely difficult circumstances. The work is not to eliminate that feeling by force, but to find the small, real places where your choices still matter — and act there.

The tools from this session — sorting what you can control versus influence versus accept, and capturing emotions without being overwhelmed by them — are practical and immediately usable. You are already taking adaptive steps. The task now is to recognize them as such.
