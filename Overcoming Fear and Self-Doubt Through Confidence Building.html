Facing Fear and Finding Confidence

Breaking Survival Mode and Building Self-Belief

The Situation

Persistent fear has narrowed your career choices, kept you in jobs that feel limiting, and made it hard to believe better opportunities are within reach. You stay busy to manage stress, but that busyness also keeps you from making space for growth or recognizing your own strengths.

Medication concerns — including appetite and weight changes on sertraline, and worry about pharmacy shortages — add an additional layer of stress. These are real, practical issues that deserve straightforward solutions alongside the emotional work.

Core Problem

The central pattern is fear-driven avoidance: the belief that failure would be catastrophic leads to not trying, which prevents learning, which reinforces the belief that you can't succeed. It becomes self-sealing.

Underneath this is a persistent mismatch — you consistently underestimate your own capability while overestimating everyone else's. Your resilience and life experience are real assets, but fear distorts the lens through which you see yourself.

Understanding the Dynamics

Survival ModeLiving in constant vigilance — focused on getting through the day rather than building toward something better. It crowds out growth and joy.
Fear-Avoidance CycleAvoiding risk to prevent failure feels safe short-term, but it reinforces the belief that you can't handle challenges, shrinking your world over time.
Rebound SymptomsA temporary increase in anxiety or low mood when reducing medication dose. This is expected, usually brief (one to two weeks), and not a sign that things are getting worse.
Medication Side EffectsSertraline can increase appetite and contribute to weight changes; Adderall tends to suppress it. Your current experience likely reflects sertraline's effect, not a personal failure of willpower.

Frameworks

1 — The Learning-Through-Mistakes Model
  • 1Accept that nobody knows everything — everyone is figuring it out as they go.
  • 2Allow yourself to make mistakes; this is how learning actually happens.
  • 3Reflect on what went wrong to gain insight, not to assign blame.
  • 4Take your next action based on what you learned, not on fear of imperfection.
How it appliesYour anxiety in interviews is normal — not evidence that you don't belong there. The goal is to learn from each attempt, not to perform flawlessly from the start.
2 — The Uniqueness Principle
  • 1Acknowledge that everyone has different strengths and gaps — no one fits a single mold.
  • 2Recognize that your differences are a source of value, not a deficit to overcome.
  • 3Redirect energy from comparison to identifying what you specifically bring to a situation.
How it appliesFeeling "too old" or different from other candidates is a comparison trap. Your lived experience and distinct perspective are genuinely useful — to the right employer, they're an advantage.

Practical Instructions

  • Reduce sertraline from 200mg to 150mg daily (one and a half tablets); take at the same time each day and monitor mood and appetite.
  • Continue Adderall 20mg each morning as prescribed; do not double up if a dose is missed.
  • Pick up prescriptions on a consistent monthly schedule and maintain a small reserve supply to reduce stress about shortages.
  • Update your resume and browse administrative assistant roles on LinkedIn — focus on what you offer, not on perceived gaps.
  • Each day, name one thing you did well or one quality you noticed in yourself — write it down or say it aloud.

Assignment

Every day until the next session, identify one positive thing about yourself — a strength you used, something you handled, or a quality you noticed. It can be small. Write it down or say it aloud at the end of the day. Come to the next session ready to share a few examples. This practice is not about forced positivity; it is about training your attention to see what's already there.

Key Takeaways

Chronic fear leads to avoidance, which keeps you stuck and reinforces self-doubt.
Mistakes are a normal, necessary part of learning — not evidence of failure.
Nobody has all the answers; you are allowed to figure things out as you go.
Your unique experiences and differences are assets, not liabilities.
Appetite changes are likely sertraline-related; a dose reduction may help without sacrificing mood stability.
Daily self-acknowledgment is a clinical tool — it builds the evidence base that counters negative self-perception.

Bottom Line

This session focused on the fear cycle that has kept you in survival mode — working constantly, avoiding risk, and interpreting your own strengths through a distorted lens. The work is to interrupt that cycle: by taking small actions, tolerating imperfection, and building a more accurate picture of what you're capable of.

Medication adjustments are in place to address appetite and weight concerns, with a clear plan for monitoring. The rest of the work happens daily — in how you talk to yourself, whether you apply for the job, and whether you let one hard moment define the whole story. You have more to offer than fear has allowed you to see.
