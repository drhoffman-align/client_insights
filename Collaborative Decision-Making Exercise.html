Collaborative Decision-Making
Exercise

Homework

The goal is not to determine who is right. The goal is to practice making decisions as a team — without having to think, process, explain, defend, and decide everything in the same conversation.

When an issue affects both of you, instead of immediately debating what should happen, pause and think separately first.

⬛ Print two copies of pages 2–3 — one for each of you to complete independently.
1

Define the Shared Goal Agree on what you're trying to accomplish — not how to accomplish it

Before discussing solutions, agree on the question you are trying to answer together. Try to reach agreement on the goal, not the solution.

Our Shared Goal

e.g., "We want to decide what to do next with the house renovation."
2

Separate and Think Each person completes this independently — do not discuss your answers while writing

Goal

What do I think we are ultimately trying to accomplish?

Resources

What do we currently have available?

Consider: money · time · people/help · existing plans · skills · available options

Considerations

What needs to be taken into account before deciding?
  • Cost
  • Stress
  • Time
  • Impact on AJ
  • Convenience
  • Impact on each other
  • Safety
  • Emotional well-being
  • Previous commitments
  • Short- & long-term consequences

Don't assume your partner has considered something because it seems obvious. Write it down.

Needs

What do I believe we genuinely need from this decision?

Wants

What would I personally prefer?

A want isn't unimportant because it isn't a need. Both people's wants matter — the purpose here is to name them clearly so you can discuss them.

3

Come Back Together Compare your answers before trying to make the decision

Compare first
→What did we agree on?
→What did you think about that I didn't?
→What did I think about that you didn't?
→Are there resources or considerations one of us missed?
→What needs do we share?
→Where are our wants different?
Then decide
→Now that we can see the whole picture — what makes sense for us to do?

The goal is not for one person to convince the other. The goal is to combine information so that the decision reflects both of you.

One Communication Rule

When you disagree with your partner's reasoning — get curious before you respond.

Instead of

"We shouldn't stop the basement and start upstairs."

Try

"If we stop the basement and start upstairs, what happens with the unfinished space downstairs?"

Instead of

"You're not considering how this affects AJ."

Try

"How do you think changing contractors or extending the renovation might affect AJ?"

Instead of

"We're going to spend more money doing it that way."

Try

"What do you think the total cost would be if we changed plans now?"

The question should be an actual question — not an accusation with a question mark attached. The purpose is to understand how your partner reached their conclusion and to surface information you may both have missed.

⚠️ Most Importantly

Do not make important collaborative decisions in the middle of an argument.

If either of you becomes frustrated, defensive, overwhelmed, or feels the need to prove your point — pause. You do not have to solve the problem right then.

"I don't think we're collaborating anymore. Let's pause, think about this separately, and come back to it."
