Alignment Psychiatry

Navigating Power, Purpose, and Values in the Workplace

Aligning Personal Ethics with Organizational Realities

The Situation

Following a merger, new leadership has introduced a profit-centered approach that stands in clear contrast to longstanding values around impact, purpose, and integrity. The shift has created friction that is hard to ignore and difficult to rationalize away.

There is also a social dimension: interactions with the CEO have not felt psychologically safe, making authentic engagement impossible. The question of whether to stay and attempt to influence the culture — or to step away — is now pressing.

Core Problem

The central issue is a deep misalignment between personal values — rooted in meaningful impact and ethical integrity — and an organizational culture that prioritizes revenue over people. This gap is generating real emotional cost: disengagement, discomfort, and a loss of authenticity at work.

Compounding this is a gap between what leadership claims to stand for (expanding healthcare access) and what appears to actually drive decisions (profit and market growth). When stated purpose and real outcomes diverge, trust erodes — and so does motivation.

Understanding the Dynamics

Power Dynamics

Authority and influence are concentrated at the top, with decisions shaped by financial priorities rather than collective well-being or shared values.

Branding vs. Reality

Organizations craft narratives of purpose and altruism that may not reflect actual motivations — distinguishing the image from the practice is essential.

Outcomes vs. Intentions

Good intentions do not guarantee good outcomes. What an organization says it wants to achieve must be evaluated against what actually happens.

Psychological Safety

When authenticity feels risky at work, it signals an environment that does not support genuine engagement — a meaningful warning sign for well-being.

Frameworks

1 — Power and Branding Framework

  1. 1Recognize how branding shapes perception and behavior inside organizations.
  2. 2Identify who holds power, how it is exercised, and toward what end.
  3. 3Assess the gap between stated values and actual organizational practices.
  4. 4Identify your own sources of leverage: expertise, relationships, specialized knowledge.
  5. 5Decide how to use that leverage — to influence change or to exit with integrity intact.
Application: The CEO's framing of healthcare as a bipartisan cause obscures profit-driven motives. Recognizing this as branding — not conviction — clarifies why decisions feel misaligned and supports clearer thinking about what is actually happening.

2 — Outcomes vs. Intentions Model

  1. 1Separate what leadership says it intends from what the data and observable outcomes show.
  2. 2Evaluate real-world impact — who is actually helped, and at what cost to whom.
  3. 3Use this analysis to make decisions grounded in evidence, not rhetoric.
Application: Framing expanded healthcare access as a mission obscures that the actual driver is customer acquisition. Holding that distinction clearly prevents self-deception and supports ethical decision-making.

Practical Instructions

  • Notice persistent discomfort or ethical conflict at work — treat it as information, not noise.
  • Continue learning the motivations, structures, and power dynamics within your organization.
  • Identify the skills and relationships you bring, and consider how to use them strategically.
  • Contact HR to inquire about FMLA and short-term disability — you are not required to explain your reasons beyond what HR needs.

Assignment

Before your next session, log into your HR portal or contact HR directly to review your company's FMLA and short-term disability policies. Note eligibility requirements, the application process, and income replacement details. Bring any questions to the next session. The goal is to understand your options clearly so that any decision about your work situation can be made from a position of knowledge, not urgency.

Key Takeaways

Persistent ethical discomfort is a valid signal that something is genuinely misaligned.

Branding shapes organizational culture and can obscure true intentions.

Intentions and outcomes are not the same — evaluate both before drawing conclusions.

Psychological safety is a prerequisite for authentic engagement at work.

FMLA and short-term disability are legitimate, underused tools that create space for clear thinking.

Protecting your integrity and well-being is not a failure — it is a sound priority.

Bottom Line

The discomfort you are experiencing is not a personal failing — it is a coherent response to a real misalignment between your values and the system you are operating in. Understanding how power and branding function, and holding the line between stated intentions and actual outcomes, gives you a clearer map of what you are dealing with.

You have agency here. The immediate task is not to decide whether to stay or leave, but to gather information, protect your well-being, and make use of the resources available to you. Thoughtful decisions made from a stable foundation are far more reliable than those made under pressure.
