Reclaiming Your Story

Processing Burnout, Betrayal, and Emotional Energy After Workplace Trauma

The Situation

You are navigating the aftermath of significant workplace trauma in teaching — including being let go despite meaningful contributions — while managing severe anxiety, panic attacks, and psychogenic non-epileptic seizures. Exhaustion and emotional numbness have made it difficult to engage with anything beyond what is required.

You are now in a new, under-resourced teaching environment with students facing their own trauma, all while carrying unresolved pain from the previous job and a longstanding pattern of undiagnosed ADHD and stress-related physical symptoms.

Core Problem

The central issue is unresolved emotional trauma from workplace betrayal and burnout. The pain has not been processed — it has been absorbed into your body and self-concept, showing up as seizures, anxiety, and a distorted sense of your own competence and worth.

You tend to internalize blame for what were systemic failures, making it difficult to validate your own experience or grieve what was genuinely lost. The gap between your actual capability and how you see yourself is wide — and closing it requires working through the story, not around it.

Understanding the Dynamics

Psychogenic Non-Epileptic Seizures

Seizure-like episodes triggered by psychological distress, not abnormal brain electrical activity. Yours are linked to unprocessed trauma and anxiety — they are a physical signal, not a neurological disorder.

Burnout

Emotional, mental, and physical exhaustion from sustained workplace stress. Numbness and withdrawal are characteristic symptoms — they are protective responses, not signs of weakness or laziness.

Validation

Recognizing your own feelings as real and justified. Without it, self-blame fills the gap — making recovery harder. Your pain does not need to be earned or proven to be legitimate.

Power and Influence in Toxic Workplaces

Institutional power is maintained through fear, reputation, and isolation of individuals. When you feel silenced or disposable, that is a feature of the system — not a reflection of your value.

Framework 1 — Emotional Energy Processing

1

Recognize that emotional energy does not disappear — it transfers into symptoms, beliefs, or behavior.

2

Identify and name the specific emotions connected to your trauma (grief, betrayal, anger, shame).

3

Tell your story — recount the events and how each one affected you, in your own words.

4

Decide what to do with the story: share it, act on it, or consciously release it.

How This Applies to You Your seizures and exhaustion are the result of emotional energy with nowhere to go. Writing your story begins to move that energy out of your body and into a form you can work with. You never allowed yourself to grieve the loss of your role or name the betrayal — this framework creates space to do that.

Framework 2 — Power and Influence Model

1

Understand that power is simply the ability to influence others' actions or beliefs.

2

Recognize how power is built and maintained: through followers, fear, or reputation.

3

Identify how it can be disrupted: by sharing stories, refusing isolation, and collective action.

4

Acknowledge that systemic barriers are real — your struggles are responses to them, not personal failings.

How This Applies to You Before this session, you attributed your burnout to personal inadequacy. Seeing how institutional power operates makes clear that you were responding rationally to a toxic system. Understanding this is not about assigning blame — it is about stopping the cycle of self-blame so healing can begin.

Practical Instructions

  • Set aside quiet, uninterrupted time to write or voice-record the story of your last teaching year.
  • Focus on specific events, how you felt in the moment, and the impact on you — structure is not required.
  • Notice any emotional or physical reactions as you write; these are useful information, not warnings to stop.
  • Share the written account with your therapist before or at the next session.

Assignment

Before your next session, write out the story of the past year (or the year prior, if that feels more pressing). Prioritize honesty over polish — include what happened, how you felt, and what you did. Bring the document to your next session or send it ahead. The goal is not a perfect narrative; it is to get the experience out of your body and into a form that can be examined and worked through together.

Key Takeaways

Unprocessed trauma transfers into physical symptoms — your seizures and exhaustion make sense.

Writing your story is a concrete first step toward moving emotional energy outward.

Naming your emotions — grief, betrayal, anger — is an act of healing, not indulgence.

Burnout and numbness are logical responses to overwork and betrayal, not character flaws.

Toxic systems use fear and isolation to maintain power; your response to that was rational.

You do not have to process this alone — support is available, and your story matters.

Bottom Line

Your anxiety, seizures, and exhaustion are not signs of weakness. They are the predictable outcome of carrying significant emotional pain without adequate support, in a system that failed you. The work of this session was to help you see that clearly — so you can stop blaming yourself for what was not your fault.

The next step is small and specific: write the story. That single act begins to move the emotional weight you have been holding entirely inside. You do not need to resolve everything at once — you need to start, and you do not have to do it alone.
