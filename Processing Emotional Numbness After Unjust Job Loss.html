Navigating Numbness and Injustice

Reclaiming Agency After Workplace Trauma

The Situation

After eight years of dedicated service, you were abruptly fired without clear justification. The sudden loss has triggered emotional numbness, persistent anxiety, sleep disruption, and difficulty managing competing life roles — professional, mother, wife, and daughter — all at once.

Your former employer's framing of events has left you questioning your own integrity despite a lack of evidence against you. This, layered on top of a pattern of feeling unsupported by leadership, has created a profound sense of powerlessness and self-doubt.

Core Problem

The central tension is between your internal reality — feeling overwhelmed, betrayed, and unsafe — and the external narrative your former employer imposed, one that challenges your integrity without basis. This gap has made it hard to advocate for yourself clearly or trust your own perception of events.

A longstanding tendency to prioritize others' needs over your own has compounded this, making it harder to set limits at work and easier to absorb blame that does not belong to you.

Understanding the Dynamics

Emotional Numbness

A protective response to emotional overload — not an absence of feeling, but too many feelings arriving at once.

Anxiety

Excessive worry paired with physical symptoms (racing heart, shaking). It often intensifies when anger has no outlet.

Power Dynamics

Workplace authority is granted by structure and resources, not by competence or morality — making it an unreliable measure of who is right.

People-Pleasing

Prioritizing others' comfort over your own needs leads to boundary erosion, overwhelm, and difficulty speaking up when it matters most.

Frameworks

Framework 1 — Emotional Numbness as Overload

  • 1Recognize numbness as a signal of emotional overflow, not emptiness.
  • 2Name the specific emotions underneath — anger, grief, fear, betrayal.
  • 3Accept that numbness is a protective mechanism, not a personal failing.
  • 4Process the emotions in small, manageable steps rather than all at once.
How this applies: Your numbness following the job loss mirrors what you experienced after childbirth and high work stress. It is your system managing overflow. Moving toward those emotions — one at a time — is how processing begins.

Framework 2 — Power Dynamics Reframe

  • 1Challenge the assumption that authority figures are inherently right or more credible.
  • 2Understand that institutional power often reflects access to resources, not merit.
  • 3Reframe dismissal as evidence of systemic dysfunction, not personal failure.
How this applies: Your employer's actions say something about how power was used, not about your character or competence. Separating those two things is essential to reclaiming a clear sense of self.

Practical Instructions

  • When numbness sets in, pause and try to name one specific emotion beneath it.
  • Begin documenting all communications and events related to your termination with dates and details.
  • Send written outreach to trusted colleagues or leadership — to inform, not to recruit support or take sides.
  • Consult your attorney brother about legal protections and next steps before the next session.

Assignment

Before the next session: draft and send a brief, factual email to a trusted colleague or the relevant VP informing them of your departure and its circumstances. Separately, write down the emotions you notice beneath the numbness — just naming them is enough. Complete initial documentation and your legal consultation as well. The goal is movement, not resolution.

Key Takeaways

Numbness is emotional overload — not weakness or indifference.

Workplace power is structural, not moral — it does not determine who is right.

Your firing reflects a failure of leadership, not a failure of your character.

Documentation creates both practical protection and psychological grounding.

People-pleasing tendencies made you vulnerable; recognizing them is the first step.

Self-validation — stating clearly what you know to be true — counteracts internalized blame.

Bottom Line

What happened to you was abrupt, unjust, and disorienting. The numbness, anxiety, and self-doubt you are carrying are proportionate responses to real harm — not signs of fragility. The goal now is not to push through the emotions but to begin moving with them, in manageable steps, toward clarity and action.

You have already demonstrated the capacity to advocate for yourself. The work ahead involves redirecting that capacity inward — toward self-protection, accurate self-perception, and grounded action — rather than absorbing blame for a situation that was not of your making.
