Alignment Psychiatry

From Proximity to Clarity

Understanding Relationship Patterns and Moving Forward

The Situation

This session focused on processing the end of a five-year marriage that began during the Covid pandemic. The relationship escalated quickly under unusual circumstances, and there was limited opportunity to see the partner's full self before major commitments were made. The client is experiencing grief, anger, and self-doubt alongside a strong desire to understand what happened and prevent similar patterns.

Reflection in session touched on how family dynamics, the partner's social circle, and the client's own history of caretaking roles all contributed to how the relationship unfolded and how the separation is being experienced now.

Core Problem

The relationship was built on proximity and hope rather than demonstrated compatibility. External pressures — including pandemic isolation and an ultimatum — accelerated commitment before there was enough real-world evidence to support it. As a result, the version of the partner the client knew was incomplete.

Compounding this was an imbalance in accountability: the client consistently took ownership of problems while his partner deflected responsibility. This pattern led to exhaustion, unmet expectations, and an inaccurate picture of who was responsible for the relationship's difficulties.

Understanding the Dynamics

Covert RelationshipA relationship that develops in isolation or unusual circumstances, masking incompatibilities and preventing genuine knowledge of the other person.
AccountabilityThe willingness to own one's role in a relationship's difficulties. Healthy relationships require this from both partners, not just one.
Emotional MaturityShown through self-reflection, effective communication, and willingness to take responsibility — absent in a partner who deflects blame.
Grief ProcessLoss of a relationship involves moving through denial, anger, bargaining, and depression toward acceptance — supported by honest self-reflection.

Frameworks

A. The Onion Model

1
People reveal themselves in layers over time, not all at once.
2
Early stages typically show only the outermost surface.
3
Seek exposure to your partner across varied settings and relationships.
4
Delay major commitments until multiple "versions" of someone have been seen.
How it applies: The client saw his partner mainly in Covid isolation — a narrow slice. Surprises emerged as more layers became visible only after marriage.

B. Proximity vs. True Connection

1
Distinguish closeness due to circumstance from genuine compatibility.
2
Assess whether connection is rooted in shared values and mutual support.
3
Ask whether the relationship would have developed the same way outside the bubble.
How it applies: "Best friend" status felt real during lockdown but was largely a product of being each other's only company. Differences became clear once life reopened.

Practical Instructions

  • Meet a partner's friends and family early — how they interact in their world reveals a great deal.
  • Look for partners who can acknowledge mistakes and accept feedback without defensiveness.
  • Notice and name red flags in real time rather than rationalizing them away.
  • Pursue partners who are established and self-sufficient, not those who need rescuing.

Assignment

Continue journaling regularly about lessons from this relationship and what you want going forward. Focus on both what went wrong and what you need in a future partner — values, accountability, emotional maturity. Bring any new patterns or insights to the next session.

Key Takeaways

External pressure, not deep compatibility, drove the relationship's pace.
Pandemic proximity created a false sense of intimacy and shared identity.
Seeing a partner in varied contexts is essential before committing.
Taking all the blame in a relationship is neither accurate nor healthy.
Emotional maturity is proven by behavior — especially under conflict.
Caretaking-oriented relationships are often unsustainable long-term.

Bottom Line

This session helped clarify how circumstance, limited exposure, and an imbalance in accountability shaped the arc of this relationship. The patterns identified — rapid escalation, isolation-based bonding, and over-functioning — are understandable given the context, and they are also changeable.

The path forward involves honest self-reflection, deliberate relationship-building, and holding future partners to a standard of mutual accountability. Healing is in progress; each insight built in this work is a meaningful step toward something more sustainable.
