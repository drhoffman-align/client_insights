Navigating Uncertainty and Power: Becoming Your Own Champion in Organizational Change

Therapeutic insights from a focused session on self-advocacy, expertise communication, and organizational transitions

The Situation

A company merger has disrupted a previously clear professional role and trajectory. New leadership structures are in place, decision-makers are difficult to identify, and the organization's vision for the life sciences function remains undefined.

The result is a state of professional limbo — expertise that was previously recognized now goes unacknowledged, and the path forward feels dependent on others' decisions rather than one's own actions.

Core Problem

The central tension is a significant mismatch between deep life sciences expertise and new leadership's limited understanding of that domain. Because leadership cannot independently assess the value of this work, that value will not be assumed — it must be demonstrated.

Waiting for clarity or recognition to arrive from the organization is a losing strategy. The expertise exists; the communication of it does not yet.

Understanding the Dynamics

Expertise Gap

Leadership's unfamiliarity with life sciences makes your contributions invisible by default, not by judgment.

Power Through Knowledge

Specialized knowledge is leverage — but only when it is named, contextualized, and connected to outcomes leadership cares about.

Structural Ambiguity

Unclear reporting lines and undefined roles create anxiety; proactive communication reduces their impact on your standing.

Ethical Alignment

Organizational change can shift norms and expectations; monitoring alignment with your professional ethics is an ongoing, active task.

Frameworks

1. The PAUA Framework

  1. PPause — resist the urge to react immediately to ambiguity or pressure
  2. AAssess — gather factual data about the new structure and key players
  3. UUnderstand — identify what has actually changed vs. what is unknown
  4. PPlan — determine targeted next steps based on what you now know
  5. AAct — initiate deliberate, purposeful communication and outreach
Application: Use PAUA before any high-stakes conversation or decision in the merger process to stay grounded and strategic rather than reactive.

2. Champion vs. Seller Model

  1. 1Champion role — defend your expertise, your team, and past results from erosion
  2. 2Seller role — proactively communicate what your work produces and why it matters
  3. 3Before/After analysis — document what existed and worked, then name what is now at risk
Application: Both roles are required simultaneously. Championing without selling leaves leadership uninformed; selling without championing leaves your past work unprotected.

Practical Instructions

  • Map the new organizational structure and identify who the actual decision-makers are for your function
  • Initiate direct, brief conversations with key stakeholders — do not wait to be approached
  • Prepare a concise before/after document showing department results, current risks, and open questions
  • Set explicit boundaries around intellectual property and monitor requests that may conflict with your ethics

Assignment

Before or during the upcoming company retreat, prepare and deliver a short presentation to leadership covering: key department achievements, your specific contributions, what is at risk under the current ambiguity, and two to three targeted questions for decision-makers. Use concrete data throughout. This is not a performance — it is a professional record and an act of self-advocacy.

Key Takeaways

The merger created the uncertainty; you are responsible for defining your path through it

Your value is not self-evident in the new structure — active communication is required

Leadership's knowledge gap makes your expertise more critical, not less relevant

PAUA gives you a repeatable process for navigating high-stakes ambiguity

Being both champion and seller is not self-promotion — it is professional responsibility

Concrete data and before/after framing make your contributions legible to any audience

Bottom Line

This is a pivotal moment. The uncertainty is real, but so is the expertise — and expertise that goes uncommunicated carries no weight in a new organization. The frameworks from this session provide a clear structure for moving from reactive to deliberate.

The goal is not to convince anyone of your worth. It is to make your contributions visible, your questions direct, and your boundaries clear — so that whatever happens next is shaped by your voice, not by others' assumptions.
